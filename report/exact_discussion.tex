The choice of timing metric changes the small-board interpretation. At $n=8$, reference SAT has median process time 0.247 s, compared with 1.510 s for CPLEX CP. Their construction-plus-solve medians are only 0.002 s and 0.040 s. Much of the end-to-end difference at this size lies outside the measured core interval. Process time remains the primary metric because the deadline includes those startup costs.

At $n=512$, CPLEX CP, CPLEX MIP, and Gurobi have median core times 0.092, 5.090, and 1.530 s, respectively. CPLEX CP is fastest for this core metric and also for process time at this particular size, whereas Gurobi has the lowest aggregate PAR-2 across the eight sizes. These are two different observations. The small CP input model alone is insufficient to prove why its engine performs well; solver preprocessing and search were not isolated experimentally.
